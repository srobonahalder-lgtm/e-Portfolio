MORPHOMETRIC ANALYSIS OF UTTARAKHAND
Introduction

Uttarakhand exhibits dramatic topographic variation, transitioning rapidly from the outer Shivalik foothills to the glaciated peaks of the Greater Himalayas. This study utilizes GIS and Digital Elevation Models (DEMs) to evaluate key linear, areal, and relief morphometric parameters across selected river basins. Quantifying drainage density, bifurcation ratios, and basin relief highlights the structural and lithological controls governing surface runoff, soil erosion susceptibility, and flash flood hazards, providing an empirical baseline for watershed prioritization and hazard mitigation in this fragile mountain terrain.

Overview

The morphometric analysis of Uttarakhand reveals pronounced terrain and hydro-geomorphic gradients across the state, with northern and higher Himalayan districts exhibiting extreme absolute and relative relief, steep slope gradients, and high ruggedness numbers, indicating deeply incised valleys, intense structural control, and severe susceptibility to mass movements and flash floods. In contrast, the southern foothill and Dun valley districts display lower relief ratios, permeable alluvial fan deposits, and distinct drainage frequencies characteristic of dynamic piedmont and fluvial plains. Overall, these variations emphasize the critical need for basin-specific micro-watershed prioritization, landslide risk zonation, and disaster-resilient land and water resource management across the fragile mountain state.

Conclusion & Key Findings

• High-Altitude Fragility: Northern Himalayan districts (Uttarkashi, Chamoli, Pithoragarh) exhibit steep relief gradients, high ruggedness, and intense dissection, marking them as high-energy catchments acutely susceptible to flash floods, slope failure, and rapid sediment yield.

• Foothill Drainage Dynamics: Southern foothill and alluvial districts (Haridwar, Udham Singh Nagar, Dehradun) display lower relief ratios and elevated Topographic Wetness Indices, reflecting deposition-dominated regimes that require focused surface runoff and drainage management.

• Web GIS Utility: Translating these multi-parameter indices into an interactive Web GIS platform converts complex spatial metrics into a direct decision-support system, facilitating targeted soil-water conservation, optimal check-dam siting, and risk-resilient land-use planning.
